# PTO Policy and Procedure Redesign: Case Study

## Project Overview

This portfolio project demonstrates the process of transforming a dense, text-heavy employee policy and procedure into structured, user-focused documentation.

The original document contains the information employees need, but presents policy requirements and procedural instructions primarily through long paragraphs. The redesign focuses on improving readability, scannability, consistency, and ease of use while preserving the intent of the original content.

## Documentation Goals

The redesign focused on:

- Separating policy requirements from procedural instructions
- Creating a clear information hierarchy
- Making important requirements easier to locate
- Converting procedural information into task-based steps
- Improving readability and scannability
- Using consistent terminology and formatting
- Reducing unnecessary repetition

## Content Analysis

Before redesigning the document, I reviewed the original content from the perspective of an employee trying to find information or complete a task.

### Issues Identified

**Limited information hierarchy**  
The original document presents most information as a series of paragraphs. Policy requirements, system instructions, manager responsibilities, and employee responsibilities are not clearly separated.

**Procedures embedded in paragraphs**  
The steps for requesting PTO are contained within narrative text. This makes it difficult for an employee to quickly identify the actions required to complete the task.

**Limited scannability**  
Important information such as advance notice expectations, approval requirements, and request statuses can be difficult to locate when scanning the document.

**Policy and procedure content combined**  
Rules governing PTO and instructions for completing PTO-related tasks are presented together rather than organized according to their purpose.

**Inconsistent presentation of requirements**  
Employee responsibilities and important conditions are distributed throughout the document instead of being grouped into clearly labeled sections.

## Redesign Approach

I reorganized the content around the information employees are most likely to need rather than following the paragraph structure of the original document.

Key changes included:

- Added Purpose and Scope sections to establish the document's intent and audience
- Separated policy requirements from task-based procedures
- Created descriptive headings to make information easier to locate
- Converted procedural instructions into numbered steps
- Used bulleted lists for requirements and reference information
- Grouped related information into dedicated sections
- Standardized terminology throughout the document
- Preserved the meaning of the source content while improving its organization and presentation

## Outcome

The redesigned document presents the same core information in a structure that better supports employees who need to find information quickly or complete a PTO-related task.

The final document provides:

- Clear separation between policy requirements and procedural instructions
- Task-based steps for requesting and managing PTO
- Descriptive headings that support scanning and navigation
- Consistent formatting for requirements, procedures, and reference information
- A structure that can be maintained and updated as policies or system processes change
